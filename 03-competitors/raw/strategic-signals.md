### Where Classic BSW demand is heading
- [Data] McKinsey expects domain (gen-4) plus zonal (gen-5) E/E architectures to exceed 75% of production by 2035, and cross-domain/zonal control units to more than triple between 2030 and 2035. ([McKinsey](https://mckinsey.com/features/mckinsey-center-for-future-mobility/our-insights/mapping-the-automotive-software-and-electronics-landscape))
- [Data] Vehicles carry about 60–100 ECUs today. MarketsandMarkets forecasts fewer than 10 "ECUs" per vehicle by 2030 (an aggressive, central-compute-centric count). Emergen still expects about 60 MCUs per SDV in 2030, with fewer but higher-value MCUs. ([MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/PressReleases/automotive-ee-architecture.asp), [Emergen](https://emergenresearch.com/market-intelligence-series/global-automotive-microcontroller-mcu-market-r-car-rh850-autosar-compliant-platform-intelligence-2025-2034))
- [Opinion] Classic is not dying, but the work is changing shape. Zonal controllers, safety islands, MCU partitions next to HPCs, sensors and actuators all still run Classic, and every legacy platform keeps needing variants, migrations (R4.x updates) and cyber retrofits for R155/GB 44495. The *number of distinct ECU projects per vehicle falls*, while *each zonal/integration project gets more complex* (multi-core, SecOC, IdsM, crypto stack, Ethernet). That favours tooling-heavy automation over headcount. Lower volume combined with higher complexity per project is exactly where AI configuration agents could take share. Adaptive and Linux/QNX on HPCs is the growth segment, and the founders' Classic-only scope caps the upside. [Opinion]
- [Opinion] **China OEMs** (BYD, Geely, NIO, Xpeng, etc.) develop much software in-house, move fast and often skip or minimise AUTOSAR on central compute. They also squeeze Western OEMs' R&D budgets by taking global share, which helps explain the 2025 ER&D contraction. KPIT says "traditional services will not work" in China. There are two openings: Chinese OEMs exporting to the EU need R155/R156/21434 compliance evidence, and Western OEMs need cost-down responses. Both favour cheaper AI-driven delivery. [Opinion / partly Data via AIM]

[Opinion] Regulation is the most durable demand driver in this whole picture. Cyber and ASPICE evidence is required no matter how cyclical engineering budgets are, and it is document-heavy, which suits LLMs. AI-generated work products must still survive assessor and technical-service scrutiny. ASPICE 4.0 MLE and growing assessor scepticism toward AI-generated evidence could become a gating risk.

## Strategic Landscape Summary

### Where the market is heading
1. **Cyclical trough meets structural shift.** Auto ER&D spending contracted sharply in 2024–25 (claimed −24–25% in 2025). Recovery in 2026 is selective (cockpit, AD, cyber, diagnostics). [Data/Opinion]
2. **From effort to outcomes.** Incumbents are cannibalising time-and-materials billing for fixed-price, AI-infused delivery (KPIT at 68% fixed price, headcount "disassociated from revenue"). The founders' AI-native model is now the incumbents' stated strategy, so it is no longer contrarian. [Data]
3. **Capital markets are pricing AI deflation into ESPs** (an 11% sector drawdown after Anthropic plugins, premium compression), while VCs fund AI-for-safety-critical-code platforms at unicorn prices (Code Metal). [Data]
4. **Regulation is the counter-cyclical floor.** R155/R156, GB 44495/44496 (2026) and ASPICE 4.0 create recurring evidence work. [Data/Opinion]
5. **Architecture consolidation.** Fewer, more complex Classic ECUs, with growth in Adaptive and HPC software. [Data/Opinion]

## Flags
**Red flags**
- **The incumbent with the most overlap is already executing the founders' thesis.** KPIT (with Qorix, ZF and Qualcomm) sells agentic-AI, fixed-price software delivery to OEMs and is decoupling headcount from revenue. "AI-native ESP" will not differentiate the founders on its own. The wedge must be narrower and provably better (for example, audit-grade BSW configuration and 21434 work products at a fraction of the cost and time).
- **The market is in a downturn.** Auto ER&D budgets fell sharply in 2025, and incumbents are cutting staff (Bertrandt −1,843, Akkodis restructuring). Selling to Tier 1s that are cutting R&D lengthens sales cycles, even though cost-down AI offers might benefit.
- **Valuation risk if positioned as services.** Public markets are de-rating ESPs on AI deflation. A services-shaped pitch will be priced like services (about 1–3x revenue).

**Yellow flags**
- Classic-only scope: demand per vehicle is consolidating, and the growth is in Adaptive and HPC software.
- Assessor and technical-service acceptance of AI-generated ASPICE/21434 evidence is unproven, and ASPICE 4.0 MLE may add scrutiny.
- Tool-vendor lock-in: BSW configuration happens inside Vector, EB and ETAS tools, whose vendors could ship native AI configuration (DATA GAP: not checked here; see the other wave agents).
- Ketryx and Embedd are adjacent AI-tool startups that could commoditise parts of the offer.
- Cairo talent competition with Valeo, Arrow/Avelabs and others.
- Market-size figures come mostly from low-quality vendor reports. Use BCG, McKinsey and JM for decks.
